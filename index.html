01

I grew up in Yazd, a city of windcatchers in the middle of the desert.

In sixth grade, a great teacher opened a door to NODET, Iran's schools for gifted students. From my class, only I walked through.

Six years there. The first three were math olympiads, contests and algorithms, mostly to prove a point to myself.

02

In tenth grade, I found music.

Classical piano first, third place in Yazd. Then my own tracks in FL Studio, and an album in eleventh grade.

Music didn't become my path. It pointed to one, the way these strings do: engineering, where sound becomes signals.

This is Lost: a teenager's experiment, kept just as it was.

03

On paper, I was headed for medicine.

In practice, I kept building things. In ninth grade, with two friends beside me, I started Bartarinha, a team math competition between our school and our sister school.

It ate almost the whole budget. First business lesson: passion lights the fire, money keeps it burning.

In the year everyone studied for the national entrance exam, I stepped off the road. A year of talking to people and digging around, and I landed on computer engineering.

A few months of studying later, I was in. University of Isfahan.
